# First value — definition and the debrief

**[M] methodology.** The *definition* ships. A given product's answer does not — that comes from the target file or from the persona's own judgement on the day.

---

## What first value is

> **The moment the product visibly does something useful with the persona's own data or intent — something that would otherwise have cost them real effort.**

**First value is NOT:**
- Seeing a dashboard
- Connecting an account (that's setup)
- Reading a welcome tour or watching a demo
- The product saying *"you're all set"*
- A populated empty state

**First value IS:**
- Output the persona would have had to produce by hand
- A visible change to something the persona cares about
- Any result that uses *their* data and solves a task they'd otherwise do themselves

**The test:** if the persona closed the tab right now, would they have got anything? *"I set it up"* is not anything.

**Who decides:** the persona, in first person, on the day. Not a checklist. If the target file names a specific first-value moment, treat it as a hypothesis to test, not a definition to satisfy — a persona who reaches the nominated moment and doesn't feel it has told you something important about the nominated moment.

---

## Benchmarks

Interpretation, not scoring — scoring is Pass 2's job with the severity rubric.

| Time from persona taking over | Reading |
|---|---|
| **< 5 min** | Healthy for consumer self-serve |
| **5–10 min** | Acceptable if the product is complex or high-priced; a problem if it isn't |
| **> 10 min** | Activation at serious risk |
| **Not reached in session** | The strongest signal the traversal can produce |

Adjust for price and complexity — both come from the target file. An enterprise tool at £500/seat earns more patience than a £5/month consumer app, and holding them to one number produces confident nonsense.

**The clock starts when the persona takes over** — after any auth wall. The wall is infrastructure and never counts against the product.

## The debrief

Asked verbatim at the end of Shape 3. Answers go in `persona-debrief.md`, **first person, as the persona.**

> **Q1 — In one sentence, what does this product do for you?**
>
> **Q2 — Name three things it can do that you'd actually use.**
>
> **Q3 — Is it worth <the price from the target file> to you, or isn't it? Why?**
>
> **Q4 — Does the product you just used match the promise that brought you here?** State the promise you arrived with (from your pre-session note), then answer *yes / close but off / no*, and say in one sentence why.

**Q4 is not asking whether the product is good.** It asks whether the product and the pitch are telling the same story. Those come apart constantly, and it is the single most useful question here — a good product that contradicts its own marketing loses people before it gets a chance.

If the target has no price, ask Q3 as: *"Would you keep using this? What would you pay, if anything?"*

## What Pass 2 does with the debrief

Recorded here so the *capture* agent understands why it is being asked — **not** so it scores anything. Capture writes the answers and stops.

**Axis A — Self-explanatory (Q1–Q3)**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pass** | Q1 accurately reflects real capability · Q2 names 3 real, distinct things · Q3 is a coherent judgement either way |
| **Partial** | Q1 vague or half-right · Q2 manages 2 · Q3 expressed as uncertainty |
| **Fail** | Q1 wrong · Q2 can't reach 3 · Q3 is *"I don't know what this does"* |

**Axis B — Promise match (Q4)**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pass** | Product and pitch tell the same story |
| **Partial** | Recognisable from the pitch, but something — copy, tone, navigation — pulls against it |
| **Fail** | The product actively contradicts what brought the persona here |

**Promise-match failures are persona-specific by definition.** The same onboarding passes for one persona and fails for another, because they arrived with different promises. Pass 2 reports the score per persona and **flags any finding whose severity flips across personas** — a flip is usually the most interesting thing in the report, and averaging it away is how it gets lost.

---

## A note on honesty

The persona is allowed to conclude the product is not for them, not worth the money, or not comprehensible. A debrief that reads like marketing copy means the persona was contaminated — check whether framework vocabulary or the product's own language leaked into the capture prompt.
