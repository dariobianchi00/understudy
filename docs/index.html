Your users don't file bug reports. They leave.

understudy sends a stand-in user through your live product — someone with a goal, a short attention span and somewhere else to be. It gets stuck where a real person gets stuck, gives up where they'd give up, and hands you a report where every finding cites a screenshot you can open.

One session. Four different questions answered.

Driving the product is the expensive part, so understudy does it once and scores that single recording through as many lenses as you asked for. Adding a question costs minutes, not another hour in the browser.

Is it usable?

Where they hesitated, backtracked, and gave up.

What's broken?

Console errors, failed requests, dead ends — with repro steps.

Where do people drop off?

Time to first value, steps to get there, the point they stopped.

Does it deliver what the marketing promised?

What they expected on arrival, against what they found.

Websites get their own set — whether a visitor can say what you do, whether the next step is obvious, whether they believe you, plus crawlability, structured data and page speed. And a comparison against competitors you name.

Two rules that make the findings worth reading.

The stand-in never sees the scoring framework.

An agent that knows what it's looking for finds it every time. So the persona is told a goal and nothing else — no heuristics, no severity scale, no previous findings. A separate reviewer scores the recording afterwards. The vocabulary is banned during the session and checked mechanically; one slip fails the run.

Every finding cites evidence, with no exceptions.

No screenshot, log line or network trace means the finding is dropped — and that holds hardest for findings that seem obviously true, because those are the ones nobody checks. Anything dropped goes in a list at the end, so there's somewhere honest to put a hunch.

What it can't tell you.

Scoring the same recording twice, with the same reviewers on the same models, reproduces about a third of the individual findings. We measured it rather than assumed it:

30.4%findings that recur when the same evidence is scored twice
42.9%recurrence among the serious ones
P0 → P1one finding scored differently between the two runs

The themes are stable. The individual findings are not. Both runs reported the same problems — the audience is never named, four features are sold and never explained, the most important capability is buried in the privacy policy. What changed was how those got grouped, worded and ranked.

So treat any single finding as one careful reading rather than a verdict, and open its evidence before you act on it. That's what the citations are for. No evaluation tool publishes this number; it's here because a report you can't calibrate is worse than no report.

Two more limits worth knowing up front. If you don't supply your own personas, understudy invents plausible ones and says so on the face of every report — inferred personas produce materially weaker findings. And it never handles a credential: at a login screen it stops and hands you the browser.

Install

You need Playwright MCP connected — understudy drives a real browser, and refuses to fall back to fetching pages over HTTP and calling it a session.

# in Claude Code
/plugin marketplace add dariobianchi00/understudy
/plugin install understudy

# then
/understudy:run https://your-product.com

It interviews you first — one question at a time — then shows you the plan and the time it will take, and waits. Reports land in ~/.understudy/ on your machine. Nothing is uploaded anywhere; there is nowhere to upload it to.
